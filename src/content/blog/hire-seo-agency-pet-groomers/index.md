**Quick answer:** To hire an SEO agency for a pet grooming business, set a bookings goal first, shortlist agencies with local service proof, ask Google's own vetting questions plus a few grooming-specific ones, keep ownership of every account, and walk away from anyone who guarantees rankings or sells reviews. This guide walks through each step, what fair pricing looks like, and the red flags worth memorizing.

The stakes are high for a local service business. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of 1,002 US adults found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses. The same survey found that 45% now use ChatGPT or other AI tools for local recommendations, up from 6% in 2025. The agency you hire shapes how your salon shows up in both places, and demand is there to win: the American Pet Products Association reports that 71 million US households owned a dog in 2025.

## Do You Need an SEO Agency, or Can You Do It Yourself?

Many small grooming businesses can handle SEO basics themselves. Google's own guide, "Do you need an SEO?", says a small local business can probably do much of the work alone, starting with Google's SEO Starter Guide.

### When Hiring an SEO Agency Pays Off

An agency earns its fee when one or more of these is true:

- You're launching or redesigning your website. Google's guide names this as the most useful time to bring in an SEO, because the site gets built search-friendly from day one.
- Competing salons beat you in the Google Maps results for your main services and towns.
- Your table has open weekday slots, and you have no regular time set aside for marketing.
- You run more than one location, or a mobile route that covers several towns.

If none of these fit, update your Google Business Profile yourself first, then revisit hiring in a few months.

## What Does an SEO Agency Do for a Grooming Business?

An SEO agency for groomers improves the places pet owners use to find and choose a groomer: your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews, and your booking path. Here is what good work looks like in each area.

| Work area | What good work looks like for a groomer |
| --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | Accurate categories, a full services list, real photos of finished grooms, current hours, regular posts |
| Service pages | One page per main service (full groom, bath and tidy, de-shedding, cat grooming, first puppy groom) and per main area served |
| Reviews | A steady, compliant review request after every appointment, plus a reply to every review |
| Local citations | The same business name, address and phone number across directories |
| Booking path | A "Book now" button on every page that opens your booking software in one step on mobile |
| Tracking | Calls, booking clicks, direction requests and form leads reported monthly |
| AI search visibility | The same fundamentals, checked against Google's guidance for its AI features |

## Storefront, Mobile or Home-Based: The First Question a Good Agency Asks

Your business model changes how your Google Business Profile is set up, so a good agency asks about it on the first call. Google's Guidelines for representing your business on Google tell service-area businesses to hide their address from customers, and they rule out virtual offices as ineligible for a profile.

### The Three Grooming Business Setups

- Storefront salon: shows its real street address.
- Mobile groomer: hides the address and sets a service area instead.
- Hybrid (salon plus van): shows the storefront address and adds a service area.

Home-based groomers who travel to clients follow the mobile setup. If an agency suggests renting a virtual office so you "rank in the next town," end the call there.

## How to Hire an SEO Agency for Your Grooming Business in 7 Steps

Hiring well takes seven steps, in this order. Step 6 matters most: skip it, and you risk losing control of your profile and website when the contract ends.

### Step 1: Set a Bookings Goal, Not a Traffic Goal

A grooming business sells a fixed number of table slots each day, so extra website visitors mean nothing if your Saturdays are already full. Before you talk to any agency, write down which services and days you want to fill. Weekday cat grooms, spring de-shedding packages and new puppy clients are all good examples. Hand that list to every agency you interview. The good ones build their plan around it.

### Step 2: Shortlist Agencies With Local Service Proof

Look for agencies that show results for local, appointment-based businesses, ideally groomers, vets, boarding or daycare. Google's hiring guide recommends asking about experience in your industry and your city, so make that a filter, not a bonus. Three agencies is enough for a shortlist.

Treat cold emails that say "we found problems on your site" with heavy skepticism. Google's guide gives the same advice about unsolicited SEO pitches.

### Step 3: Ask the Right Interview Questions

Use Google's core vetting questions, then add grooming-specific ones. Pay attention to what the agency asks you back, too. Google's guide says a good SEO shows interest in your business: what makes it different, who your competitors are, and who your customers are.

#### Questions From Google's Hiring Guide

These come straight from Google's guide:

- Can you show examples of past work and success stories?
- Do you follow Google Search Essentials?
- What results should I expect, over what timeframe, and how will you measure success?
- How long have you been in business?
- Will you share every change you make and the reasoning behind it?

#### Questions Specific to Pet Grooming

- Is my business a storefront, a service-area business or a hybrid, and what does that change on my profile?
- How will you track bookings that come through my online booking software?
- What's your plan for getting more reviews without breaking the FTC's rules on reviews?
- Which services and towns will you target first, and why those?

### Step 4: Check References and Real Results

Ask each agency for two or three current clients you can call. Google suggests asking past clients whether the SEO was useful, easy to work with and got positive results. Look for results tied to calls and bookings, not ranking screenshots alone. A top ranking for a phrase nobody searches books zero appointments.

#### Give Audit Access Without Giving Control

If an agency offers a free audit, give it restricted access in Google Search Console. Google's hiring guide says to grant read access only at the audit stage, and a restricted user in Search Console has view rights on most data without tools like sitemap submission or URL removal.

### Step 5: Compare SEO Pricing and Contract Terms

SEO providers price by monthly retainer, hourly rate or project fee. Compare quotes only after you know what each one covers.

#### What SEO Agencies Charge

In Ahrefs' poll of 439 SEO providers, published in December 2023, the most common retainer was $501 to $1,000 per month (20.4% of respondents), and 68.8% charged $2,000 or less. Agencies averaged about $3,209 per month against about $1,349 for freelancers, though Ahrefs built those averages from the top of each price band. The poll covers providers worldwide, not grooming specialists, so read it as a market range rather than a groomer benchmark.

#### Contract Terms to Get in Writing

- Contract length and notice period
- A list of monthly deliverables
- Who owns the content, pages and photos if you leave
- Whether any ad spend is billed separately from the SEO fee

A cheap plan built on spammy links isn't a bargain. Google's guide warns that aggressive tactics that break its spam policies can lower a site's presence in Google or remove it from the index.

### Step 6: Keep Ownership of Every Account

You own your accounts, and the agency borrows access. Set it up like this before any work starts.

| Asset | Role you keep | Role the agency gets | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile | Primary owner | Manager | A profile has only one primary owner, and managers can't add or remove users or delete the profile |
| Google Search Console | Verified owner | Full user (restricted during an audit) | Full users can't add or remove users or owners |
| Google Analytics 4 | Administrator | Editor or Marketer | In Google Analytics, only Administrators can add or remove users |
| Domain name | Registered in your name and email | Technical access only, if needed | Whoever controls the domain controls the website |
| Website and hosting | Account in your name | Its own user login | You keep the site if the agency relationship ends |
| Content and photos | Yours, stated in the contract | Permission to publish | No dispute over who owns your pages |

Google's hiring guide also says business owners remain responsible for the actions of the companies they hire. Knowing exactly who can touch what is how you protect yourself.

### Step 7: Agree on Reporting and a 90-Day Review

Agree on a [monthly report](/how-to-create-effective-client-reports-with-brightlocal/) built around calls, booking-button clicks, direction requests, new reviews and rankings for your main services in your towns. Then put a 90-day review in the contract. At 90 days, you're checking that the promised work shipped and that the early numbers are moving, not judging the final result.

## Red Flags When Hiring an SEO Agency

Each red flag below traces back to a written Google or FTC rule. One is enough to walk away.

- **Guaranteed #1 rankings.** Google's hiring guide states that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google.
- **A "special relationship" with Google.** Google warns about SEOs who claim special access or a "priority submit," and says it never accepts payment to rank sites.
- **Review packages.** The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews bans buying fake reviews and offering incentives tied to positive or negative sentiment, and it lets the FTC seek civil penalties against knowing violators.
- **Keywords stuffed into your business name.** Google's guidelines say your profile name reflects your real-world name, and adding unnecessary information can lead to suspension.
- **A virtual office in another town.** Google's guidelines say a virtual office isn't eligible for a Business Profile.
- **Secrecy about tactics.** Google's guide says to be cautious of any company that won't clearly explain what it plans to do.
- **Link schemes or "submit to thousands of search engines."** Google's guide names both as tactics to avoid.
- **The agency owns your profile or domain.** See Step 6. Access gets shared, ownership doesn't.

## What About AI Search, GEO and AEO?

For Google, optimizing for AI search is SEO. Google's guide to optimizing for generative AI features says SEO best practices continue to apply, and it states that Google Search doesn't use llms.txt files or special markup. The same guide notes that Google Business Profiles can help your services appear in AI responses.

### How to Judge a GEO or AEO Package

AI tools are now a real discovery channel for local services, given BrightLocal's finding that 45% of US consumers use them for local recommendations. Google's hiring guide now tells business owners to check that any "AEO" or "GEO" advice lines up with Google's official guidance. So if an agency charges a separate GEO package to add llms.txt files to your grooming site, ask what that does for Google Search. According to Google, nothing, because Search doesn't use those files.

One caveat: Google's guidance covers Google's own AI features, not ChatGPT or other assistants. Ask any agency how it checks whether your salon gets recommended in those tools, and what it changes as a result.
